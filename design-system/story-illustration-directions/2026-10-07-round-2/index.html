Direction 1

Living Lines

A drawing that feels observed, rather than assembled from symbols.

Why it fits

Fine botanical detail gives the growing story specificity. The light ink treatment leaves room for the existing editorial typography.

Keep it disciplined

Detailed stems and gills need simplifying at mobile size; avoid antique engraving and scientific specimen labels.

Motion direction

One quiet 360ms reveal; later, a few separately drawn stems could resolve once as the chapter enters. Never animate the entire garden continuously.

Direction 2

Layers of Growth

Small growing scenes become tactile, carefully constructed objects.

02 · The curiosity

Then, curiosity moved indoors.

Microgreens and mushrooms came next, grown inside our home. Experimenting with these crops opened up possibilities beyond the vegetables we started with.

A new crop. Another question.

Why it fits

Layered leaves and mushroom caps echo the brand’s tactile character while bringing the greatest visual change from the current icon scenes.

Keep it disciplined

Keep the relief shallow and the shadows restrained; otherwise it can feel like a toy or a separate 3D brand.

Motion direction

A short 360ms reveal. A final layered version could settle its foreground by 4px once; no pointer tilt or permanent floating.

How the full story would unfold

  1. 01

    The terrace

    One paper planter, a folded climbing stem and a barely raised terrace ledge.

  2. 02

    The curiosity

    Folded microgreen leaves and layered oyster mushroom fans share a shallow growing shelf.

  3. 03

    The purpose

    An open paper produce crate expands around the tiny original planter, with one folded leaf extending past its edge.

  4. 04

    The brand

    Keep the supplied logo, caption, crop motif and existing composition unchanged.

  5. 05

    The next chapter

    A shallow cream paper plate receives sculpted produce; soft contact shadows supply depth.

Direction 3 · Recommended

The Growing Still Life

Warm, expressive still lifes make each chapter feel like an edition of the harvest.

02 · The curiosity

Then, curiosity moved indoors.

Microgreens and mushrooms came next, grown inside our home. Experimenting with these crops opened up possibilities beyond the vegetables we started with.

A new crop. Another question.

Why it fits

A strong match for Cultivated Editorial: appetizing produce, tactile pigment and generous empty space, without competing with the logo.

Keep it disciplined

Brush texture must stay selective; full painted backgrounds or rustic props would make the page too busy.

Motion direction

A single 360ms reveal of the completed illustration. If layered later, reveal just a small painted shadow; no looping plant growth.

How the full story would unfold

  1. 01

    The terrace

    One terracotta pot with a modest vegetable plant; a brush of light suggests the terrace.

  2. 02

    The curiosity

    A still life of microgreen trays and oyster mushroom fans, with a softly suggested indoor ledge.

  3. 03

    The purpose

    An open harvest crate gathers the two crops; one small tray remains beside it to show the shift in scale.

  4. 04

    The brand

    Keep the supplied logo, caption, crop motif and existing composition unchanged.

  5. 05

    The next chapter

    A simple ceramic plate with a restrained arrangement of fresh produce, ready for the next chapter.
